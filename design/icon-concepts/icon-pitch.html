Rewards Tracker · Icon pitch

Four directions for a new app icon

Four designers, four distinct briefs, all vector-first and built on the app's crimson brand colour. Each concept is shown at App Store scale, then at home-screen (64), toolbar (32), and favicon (16) sizes on light and dark grounds — the sizes where icons actually live.

The current icon — a skeuomorphic coin stack with a clock face on teal. It has the tell-tale AI-illustration look (glossy gradients, arbitrary detail), the clock says "time tracking" rather than rewards, and the teal doesn't match the app's crimson UI. All four proposals below fix those three things.

A

Card & Spark

Geometric minimal

A white credit card tilted eight degrees on the brand crimson, chip knocked out in red, with a single four-point spark off the top corner — the "earn something extra" story in four shapes.

For: the most literal — instantly reads as "credit card rewards". Safe, versatile, ships as-is. Against: card motifs are common across fintech, so it's the least ownable of the four.

B

Rebound R

Monogram

A custom-drawn "R" whose leg dips to the baseline and rebounds up-right into a trend arrow — spend goes out, value comes back. Single-weight rounded stroke on a quietly gradated crimson field.

For: the most brand-like and ownable; the letterform itself tells the rewards story. Against: an "R" alone says nothing about cards; the arrow joint deserves one refinement pass before shipping.

C

Kinetic Ring

Progress gauge

A 75%-complete progress arc with a red-to-ember gradient over a faint track, anchored by a crimson nucleus — the app's minimum/maximum-spend mechanic distilled to one gesture, on near-black.

For: maps directly to what the app actually does (spend progress and caps); premium, Apple-Fitness feel. Against: rings are a crowded genre (fitness, habit apps), and the dark tile is quieter on dark home screens.

D

Reward Spark

Playful bold

One chunky four-point spark — the universal "you earned something" glyph — white on solid crimson with a tiny echo spark, Cash App boldness with fully symmetric geometry.

For: the simplest and most legible at 16px; strong shelf presence. Against: the sparkle glyph has become shorthand for "AI feature" — given the brief was to look less AI-generated, that association may work against it.
